A self-destructing pop star runs from Hollywood to Greenwich Village to find out what happened to her father, a legendary protest poet who vanished in 1974, and finds the truth has been hiding in plain sight.

The story

A father who vanished. A daughter who can't stop running.

1974. Tony, the voice of a protest generation, vanishes from a locked psychiatric ward. All they find is blood on an empty bed.

1995. His daughter Chelsea Moore is the world's best-selling bad-girl pop star, and she is coming apart. She runs to the Village her parents left behind.

His oldest friends are still there: Frankie, a Pulitzer-winning beat poet, and Johnny, a jazz legend who preaches in Harlem. They know why Tony disappeared, and what he saw in a Los Angeles hotel kitchen in June 1968. They aren't talking. The ghosts of Bleecker Street are.

Budget scenarios · producer's estimate, November 2026

Three ways to make it. Two decisions move the number.

Build the Village on a New York stage or shoot its streets, and cast a name for Frankie or not. Pick a scenario to see its top sheet.

Priced at 2026 rates. Includes a full post schedule, a casting account, and the accounts a musical needs: choreography and dancers, music pre-records, and a real music budget.

The New York credit is estimated at the 30% base rate on qualified New York spend: 85% of production and post, plus 85% of director and cast wages, capped at 40% of other qualified costs. Every scenario shoots at least 10% of its days at a qualified New York production facility. Facility eligibility and program funding are unconfirmed, and the credit arrives after the shoot, not as upfront cash.

Facility rent and street-location access are planning allowances, not quotes. Financing fees are excluded. A line producer’s breakdown replaces these figures before any commitment.

The $8,000 facility day covers occupancy, basic utilities and facility support; equipment, set building and crew labor stay in their own accounts. Prep, hold and strike are rental days, not photography days. The $10,000 street day is a property-access allowance; permits and street control stay in account 3600. No facility has been selected or quoted.

New York requires at least 10% of principal photography days at a qualified production facility, on a set built for the production, for budgets over $15M, and at least one full day for independent productions of $15M or less. A New York City facility used by a production over $15M must meet the state's Level 2 facility specs. Full Bleecker moves four of its 40 days onto a stage; it does not add days.

Insurance is 1.5% of above the line, production and post. Bond is 2% of subtotal. Contingency is 10% of production, post and other. No scoring credit, Production Plus or upstate uplift is assumed.
